Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How much space do these flies have?
A: The flies are housed in a transparent plastic cylinder cup enclosure measuring approximately 5.5 inches in height, and 3.5 inches in diameter, providing a total volume of 53 cubic inches. This space allows for natural flight patterns, exploration behaviors, and sufficient room for multiple flies to coexist without overcrowding. The enclosure dimensions were specifically chosen to balance observational accessibility with the flies' need for movement space.
Q: Are the flies being harmed? Are they being starved?
A: The flies are under constant observation with their well-being as the top priority. They are provided with a bottle cap filled with fresh sugar water daily, which serves as their primary nutritional source meeting their caloric needs. Additionally, small pieces of fruit are periodically introduced to supplement their diet with natural sugars and nutrients. The enclosure is monitored regularly for any signs of distress, health issues, or inadequate resources. No harmful procedures are conducted; this is purely an observational study of natural fly behavior in a controlled environment.
Q: How long do they live and what happens if they die?
A: Lifespan calculations are currently being compiled as I observe them through their natural life cycles. Based on current data, the average lifespan appears to be approximately 8.5 hours, though individual variation is significant. If a fly dies within the enclosure, a complete cleanout and sterilization protocol is initiated to maintain hygienic conditions for any remaining inhabitants. This ensures that the death does not negatively impact the living flies and allows for proper documentation of the end-of-life process. The enclosure is then thoroughly cleaned before any new subjects are introduced.
